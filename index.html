Body & Aerodynamics

The 2024 Hyundai Tucson features a bold, modern SUV body with sharp lines, a wide front grille, and a distinctive shape. Its body is designed to balance strength, passenger space, and aerodynamic performance. When the car moves, it collides with air particles, creating air resistance (drag) that acts in the opposite direction to its motion. The aerodynamic shape helps guide airflow around the vehicle, reducing unnecessary turbulence. Air resistance increases with speed and can be described approximately by the equation: Fᵈ = ½ρCᵈAv² where Fᵈ is the drag force, ρ is air density, Cᵈ is the drag coefficient, A is the frontal area, and v is the car's speed relative to the air. This equation shows that, under the same conditions, doubling the speed produces approximately four times the air resistance. Lower air resistance can reduce the energy needed to maintain speed and improve fuel efficiency. The Tucson's relatively high SUV body provides useful interior space, although its larger frontal area can increase drag compared with a smaller, lower vehicle. Its tyres also experience rolling resistance, while friction between the tyres and road provides the grip needed for acceleration, braking, and cornering. Together, these physical forces influence the car's performance, stability, and energy consumption.
